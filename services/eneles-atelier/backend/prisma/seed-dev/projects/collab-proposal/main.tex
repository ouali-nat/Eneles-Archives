\section{Summary}

We propose a one-day workshop on sequence models whose cost grows slowly with the length of the input: sparse and low-rank attention, state-space models, and retrieval-based alternatives. The workshop will bring together people who design these models with people who need them for long documents, genomes, and time series.

\section{Topics}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Sparse, low-rank, and kernel approximations of attention
  \item Recurrent and state-space alternatives to attention
  \item Benchmarks that measure whether long contexts are actually used
  \item Hardware-aware implementations and memory-efficient training
\end{itemize}

\section{Format}

Four invited talks of 30 minutes, two poster sessions, six contributed talks of 12 minutes, and a closing panel. We expect about 60 submissions and 150 attendees.

\section{Organizers}

\textbf{Aria Nakamura} works on numerical methods for machine learning. \textbf{Razz} is completing a thesis on spectral methods for large graphs. \textbf{Kenji Watanabe} builds benchmarks for long-context language models.
