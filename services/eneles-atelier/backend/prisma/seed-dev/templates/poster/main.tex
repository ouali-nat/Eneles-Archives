\begin{center}
  {\Huge \textbf{Block-Sparse Attention for Long-Document Summarization}} \\[0.5em]
  {\Large Razz, Kenji Watanabe, and Aria Nakamura} \\[0.2em]
  {\large Institute for Computational Linguistics}
\end{center}
\vspace{1em}

\begin{multicols}{2}

\block{Problem}
Self-attention costs $O(n^2)$ in the sequence length $n$, so summarizers truncate long documents and never read most of them.

\block{Idea}
Each token attends to a local window and to the summaries of the $r$ blocks it scores highest:
\[
  s_{ij} = \frac{q_i^\top \bar{k}_j}{\sqrt{d}} .
\]
The selection is trained end to end with a relaxed top-$r$ operator.

\block{Results}
\begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}

    Model & arXiv & PubMed \\
    \midrule
    BART & 39.8 & 40.1 \\

    Ours & \textbf{41.6} & \textbf{42.3} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}

\block{Conclusion}
Learned global connections beat fixed ones at the same memory budget.
