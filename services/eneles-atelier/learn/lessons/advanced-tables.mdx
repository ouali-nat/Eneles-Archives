# Advanced tables

After this page you can merge cells, wrap long text inside a column, adjust spacing, shade rows, let a table run over several pages, and turn a wide table on its side.

## Cells that span columns and rows

`\multicolumn{n}{spec}{text}` replaces `n` cells with one, formatted by its own column letter. `\cmidrule{a-b}` from `booktabs` draws a rule under columns `a` to `b` only. For a cell that spans rows, the `multirow` package provides `\multirow{n}{*}{text}`: put it in the first row and leave the cells below it empty.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
  \toprule
      &            & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Hours} \\
  \cmidrule(l){3-4}
  Lab & Instrument & 2023 & 2024 \\
  \midrule
  \multirow{2}{*}{North} & Microscope & 40 & 52 \\
                         & Centrifuge & 12 & 9  \\
  \addlinespace
  South                  & Microscope & 25 & 31 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```

The `(l)` after `\cmidrule` trims the rule slightly on the left, which keeps neighboring rules apart. Use `(r)` or `(lr)` for the other sides. `\multicolumn{1}{c}{text}` is also the way to change the alignment of a single cell, such as a centered header over a right-aligned column.

## Columns that wrap

`l`, `c`, and `r` columns never break a line, so one long cell makes the whole table too wide. A `p{4cm}` column is 4 cm wide and wraps its text. With the `array` package, `m{4cm}` and `b{4cm}` do the same but align the cell with its row at the middle or the bottom instead of the top.

Working out widths by hand is tedious. The `tabularx` package takes the total width of the table and shares what is left among the `X` columns.

```latex-document
\documentclass[varwidth=11cm,border=10pt]{standalone}
\usepackage{booktabs}
\usepackage{tabularx}
\begin{document}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
  \toprule
  Term  & Meaning \\
  \midrule
  Float & An object that LaTeX may move to a place
          where it fits, such as a figure or a table. \\
  Rule  & A straight line. In a table, rules separate
          the header from the body. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{document}
```

Narrow wrapped columns are justified, which leaves wide gaps between words. Set them ragged right by putting a declaration in front of the column: `>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4cm}` or `>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X`.

## Spacing

Two settings control the density of every table. `\tabcolsep` is the padding on each side of a column (6 pt by default), and `\arraystretch` multiplies the row height. Change them inside braces to keep the effect local.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{lr}
  Oak   & 31 \\
  Maple & 7  \\
\end{tabular}
\qquad
{\setlength{\tabcolsep}{14pt}%
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}%
\begin{tabular}{lr}
  Oak   & 31 \\
  Maple & 7  \\
\end{tabular}}
```

For one row, add space after it with an optional length on the row end, `\\[4pt]`, or use `\addlinespace` from `booktabs`.

## Color

Load `xcolor` with the `table` option. `\rowcolor` shades one row, `\cellcolor` one cell, and `\rowcolors{2}{gray!12}{white}` colors odd rows gray and even rows white from row 2 onward.

```latex-document
\documentclass[border=10pt]{standalone}
\usepackage[table]{xcolor}
\begin{document}
\rowcolors{2}{gray!12}{white}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
  \rowcolor{blue!20}
  Month    & In  & Out \\
  January  & 120 & 95  \\
  February & 80  & \cellcolor{orange!40}140 \\
  March    & 110 & 102 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{document}
```

Use shading sparingly, and not together with `booktabs` rules: the space around those rules stays white and cuts through the color.

## Tables longer than a page

A `tabular` cannot break across pages. The `longtable` package provides an environment that can. It replaces both `table` and `tabular`, takes its own `\caption`, and lets you define what is repeated at each page break.

```latex-document
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage[paperwidth=9cm,paperheight=6.5cm,margin=8mm]{geometry}
\usepackage{booktabs}
\usepackage{longtable}
\begin{document}
\begin{longtable}{@{}lr@{}}
  \caption{Rainfall in June.} \\
  \toprule
  Day & Rain (mm) \\
  \midrule
  \endfirsthead
  \toprule
  Day & Rain (mm) \\
  \midrule
  \endhead
  \midrule
  \multicolumn{2}{r@{}}{\emph{continued}} \\
  \endfoot
  \bottomrule
  \endlastfoot
  1 June & 0.0 \\
  2 June & 2.4 \\
  3 June & 11.8 \\
  4 June & 0.6 \\
  5 June & 0.0 \\
  6 June & 7.1 \\
  7 June & 3.3 \\
  8 June & 0.0 \\
  9 June & 5.9 \\
  10 June & 1.2 \\
\end{longtable}
\end{document}
```

Everything before `\endfirsthead` opens the table, and everything before `\endhead` opens each later page. `\endfoot` closes every page except the last, which `\endlastfoot` closes. The rows follow. The page here is made small with `geometry` so that the break shows.

<Callout type="note" title="longtable needs more than one compile">
  The column widths are carried from one compile to the next, so a `longtable` can look uneven until TeX has run again. Eneles runs the extra passes for you.
</Callout>

## Wide tables

To save width, turn the header cells with `\rotatebox` from `graphicx`.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
  \toprule
  Student & \rotatebox{60}{Algebra}
          & \rotatebox{60}{Geometry}
          & \rotatebox{60}{Statistics} \\
  \midrule
  Amira   & A & B & A \\
  Jonas   & B & B & C \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```

If the table is still too wide, give it a landscape page. The `landscape` environment of the `pdflscape` package starts a new page, sets its content sideways, and turns that page in the PDF viewer so that it reads normally on screen.

```latex
\usepackage{pdflscape}

\begin{landscape}
  \begin{table}
    \centering
    \caption{Results for all twelve sites.}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrrrrrr}
      % a wide table
    \end{tabular}
  \end{table}
\end{landscape}
```

## Common mistakes

- **`\raggedright` in a column without `\arraybackslash`.** `\raggedright` changes what `\\` means, so the row end fails with `! Misplaced \noalign.` or an extra-alignment error. Always write `>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}`.
- **A `\multicolumn` that loses a rule or `@{}`.** Its `spec` replaces the column's whole definition for that cell. Repeat any `|` or `@{}` the column had, as in `\multicolumn{2}{r@{}}{…}`.
- **A `longtable` inside `table`.** A float cannot break across pages, so the table runs off the bottom. Use `longtable` on its own.
- **Shrinking a table with `\resizebox`.** It fits, but the text is a different size from the rest of the document. Wrap text, abbreviate headers, or rotate the table instead.
