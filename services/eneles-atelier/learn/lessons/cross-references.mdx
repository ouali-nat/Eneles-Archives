# Cross-references

After this page you can refer to any numbered part of a document by name and let LaTeX fill in the number, the page, and a clickable link.

## Label and reference

Never type a number such as "Section 3" by hand. Mark the target with `\label{name}` and print its number with `\ref{name}`. When you add, remove, or reorder material, every reference follows.

```latex-example
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
A spring that is stretched by a distance $x$ from its
rest length stores the energy
\begin{equation}
  E = \tfrac{1}{2} k x^2 . \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:method} derives
equation~\eqref{eq:energy} on page~\pageref{eq:energy}.
```

`\pageref` prints the page the label is on. `\eqref` from `amsmath` adds the parentheses that equations are cited with. The `~` is a space that cannot break, so the word and its number stay on one line.

## What a label points to

A label has no type of its own. It records the number most recently assigned at the place where it stands, so its position decides what you get.

| To refer to | Put `\label` |
| --- | --- |
| A chapter or section | Directly after the heading command. |
| An equation, or one row of `align` | Inside the environment, on that row. |
| A figure or table | After `\caption`, inside the float. |
| An item of an `enumerate` list | After `\item`. |
| A theorem, lemma, or definition | After `\begin` of the environment. |
| A footnote | Inside the footnote text. |

```latex-example
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:compact}
Every bounded sequence of real numbers has a
convergent subsequence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pick a bounded sequence. \label{step:pick}
  \item Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:compact}.
  \item Return to step~\ref{step:pick}.
\end{enumerate}
```

Things without a number, such as `\section*` or `equation*`, cannot be referred to with `\ref`. A label placed there silently takes the last number assigned before it.

## Why it takes two compiles

TeX reads a document once from top to bottom, so at a `\ref` it may not have seen the label yet. Each compile therefore writes all labels to the `.aux` file, and the next compile reads them back. After the first pass a reference prints as `??`. After the second it is correct.

```latex-example
The setup is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:setpu}.
```

In Eneles you do not rerun anything yourself: a compile repeats TeX until the references settle. A `??` that remains, as above, means the label does not exist, usually because of a typo. To avoid that, type `\ref{` and pick from the list of every label in the project. See [References and citations](/docs/references#complete-labels).

## Names and links with hyperref

Load `hyperref` and every reference becomes a link to its target. It also adds `\autoref`, which prints the kind of thing together with the number, and `\nameref`, which prints the title.

```latex-example
\section{Calibration}\label{sec:calibration}
The gain of the amplifier is the ratio of the voltage
at its output to the voltage at its input,
\begin{equation}
  g = V_{\text{out}} / V_{\text{in}} . \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
The gain in \autoref{eq:gain} is introduced in
\autoref{sec:calibration}, ``\nameref{sec:calibration}''.
```

The names come from commands that you can redefine, for example `\renewcommand{\sectionautorefname}{Section}`.

## Smarter references with cleveref

The `cleveref` package goes further. `\cref` works out the name from the kind of target, accepts several labels at once, and sorts and compresses them into ranges. `\Cref` is the form for the start of a sentence.

```latex-document
\documentclass[varwidth=11cm,border=10pt]{standalone}
\usepackage{amsmath}
\usepackage{hyperref}
\usepackage{cleveref}
\begin{document}
\begin{equation} m = \rho V \label{eq:mass}  \end{equation}
\begin{equation} F = m a    \label{eq:force} \end{equation}
\begin{equation} W = F d    \label{eq:work}  \end{equation}

\Cref{eq:mass} gives the mass. The work follows
from \cref{eq:work,eq:mass,eq:force}, and
\cref{eq:mass,eq:work} share a factor.
\end{document}
```

<Callout type="note" title="Load cleveref last">
  `cleveref` must be loaded after `hyperref`, and `hyperref` after almost everything else. In the wrong order the links or the names are lost. The `noabbrev` option prints "equation" and "figure" instead of "eq." and "fig."
</Callout>

## Name your labels

Keep labels to letters, digits, colons, and hyphens. The widespread convention is a prefix for the kind of target, then a short description of the content.

| Prefix | Target | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ch:`, `sec:` | Chapter, section | `sec:method` |
| `fig:`, `tab:` | Figure, table | `fig:rainfall` |
| `eq:` | Equation | `eq:energy` |
| `thm:`, `lem:`, `def:` | Theorem, lemma, definition | `thm:compact` |

Name the content, not the position: `fig:3` is wrong as soon as another figure is added before it. LaTeX ignores the prefix, so it is there only to help you and your co-authors.

## Common mistakes

- **`??` in the PDF.** The log says ``LaTeX Warning: Reference `fig:setpu' on page 1 undefined``. Correct the name, or add the missing `\label`.
- **The same label twice.** ``LaTeX Warning: Label `eq:energy' multiply defined`` appears, and references may point to the wrong target. This often comes from copying a figure or an equation. Rename one.
- **The wrong number.** A reference to a figure prints the section number when `\label` comes before `\caption` or sits outside the float. Move it after the caption.
- **A line break between word and number.** Write `Figure~\ref{fig:rainfall}`, not `Figure \ref{fig:rainfall}`.
