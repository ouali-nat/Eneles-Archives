# Tables

After this page you can build a table with `tabular`, rule it cleanly with `booktabs`, give it a caption and a number, and line up numbers on the decimal point.

## A first table

The `tabular` environment takes one argument that declares the columns: one letter per column. In the body, `&` moves to the next column and `\\` ends the row.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{lcr}
  Item     & Unit      & Price \\
  Notebook & each      & 3.50  \\
  Pencils  & box of 12 & 4.20  \\
\end{tabular}
```

Spaces around `&` are ignored, so line the source up however you find readable. A `tabular` is a box like a letter: it sits in the paragraph where you put it and never breaks across pages.

## Column types

| In the argument | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `l`, `c`, `r` | A column aligned left, centered, or aligned right, as wide as its widest cell. |
| `p{3cm}` | A column of fixed width whose text wraps into lines. |
| `\|` | A vertical rule at this position. |
| `@{text}` | Put `text` here instead of the normal space between columns. `@{}` removes the space. |
| `*{4}{c}` | Repeat: the same as `cccc`. |

Wrapping columns and cells that span several columns are covered in [Advanced tables](/learn/advanced-tables).

## Rules

Plain LaTeX draws horizontal rules with `\hline` and vertical ones with `|`. The result is a grid with text pressed against the lines. The `booktabs` package gives three rules with proper space around them: `\toprule`, `\midrule`, and `\bottomrule`.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{|l|r|}
  \hline
  Station & Depth (m) \\
  \hline
  Alder   & 12 \\
  Birch   & 148 \\
  \hline
\end{tabular}
\qquad
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
  \toprule
  Station & Depth (m) \\
  \midrule
  Alder   & 12 \\
  Birch   & 148 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```

The second table follows the practice of printed books: no vertical rules, a heavier rule at the top and bottom, and a lighter one under the header. The eye follows aligned columns without any help, so the vertical rules only add clutter. `@{}` at both ends removes the padding so that the rules stop exactly at the text.

Inside a long table, separate groups of rows with `\addlinespace` instead of more rules.

```latex-example
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
  \toprule
  Group & Species & Count \\
  \midrule
  Birds   & Wren     & 14 \\
          & Dunnock  & 9  \\
  \addlinespace
  Mammals & Hedgehog & 2  \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
```

## The table float

A `tabular` has no caption or number. For those, put it inside a `table` environment, which floats exactly like a figure: the same placement letters and the same `\caption` and `\label` rules apply (see [Figures and captions](/learn/figures-and-captions)).

```latex-document
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage[paperwidth=9cm,paperheight=5.5cm,margin=8mm]{geometry}
\usepackage{booktabs}
\usepackage{caption}
\pagestyle{empty}
\begin{document}
\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Boiling points at sea level.}
  \label{tab:boiling}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
    \toprule
    Liquid  & Kelvin \\
    \midrule
    Water   & 373 \\
    Ethanol & 351 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:boiling} lists both liquids.
\end{document}
```

The `geometry` line only makes the page small enough to show here. By convention a table's caption goes above it. The standard classes space captions for the position below, so a caption above sits too close to the table. Loading the `caption` package corrects that spacing.

In Eneles, press `Shift + Space`, open **Structure**, and choose **Table** to insert a table float to fill in.

## Align numbers

Right-align whole numbers with `r` so that units sit under units. Decimals with different numbers of digits need more: the `S` column of the `siunitx` package lines them up on the decimal point.

```latex-document
\documentclass[varwidth=11cm,border=10pt]{standalone}
\usepackage{booktabs}
\usepackage{siunitx}
\begin{document}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lS[table-format=3.2]S[table-format=1.3]@{}}
  \toprule
  Sample & {Mass (g)} & {Error} \\
  \midrule
  A & 4.5    & 0.25  \\
  B & 112.75 & 0.125 \\
  C & 38     & 0.5   \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{document}
```

`table-format=3.2` reserves room for three digits before the point and two after, which also centers the column under its header. Anything in an `S` column that is not a number, such as a header, must be wrapped in braces.

<Callout type="note" title="Every row ends with \\, including the last one before a rule">
  `\hline`, `\midrule`, and `\bottomrule` must come directly after a `\\`. Leave it out and TeX stops with `! Misplaced \noalign.`
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **More cells than columns.** `! Extra alignment tab has been changed to \cr.` means a row has more `&` than the column argument allows. Count the columns, or look for a missing `\\` on the row above.
- **A bare `&` or `%` in a cell.** `&` starts a new column and `%` comments out the rest of the line, including the `\\`. Write `\&` and `\%`.
- **Vertical rules with `booktabs`.** The rules have space above and below, so `|` leaves visible gaps. Drop the vertical rules.
- **A table wider than the page.** The log reports an `Overfull \hbox` and the table runs into the margin. Use a wrapping column, as shown in [Advanced tables](/learn/advanced-tables).
