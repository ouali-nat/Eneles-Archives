# Figures and captions

After this page you can give an image a numbered caption, control where LaTeX places it, put two images side by side, and print a list of figures.

## The figure environment

A `figure` is a container with a caption and a number. It is a float: LaTeX moves it to a spot where it fits, usually the top of a page, instead of leaving a half-empty page where you typed it.

```latex-example
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{example-image}
  \caption{Daily rainfall at the field station.}
  \label{fig:rainfall}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:rainfall} shows the raw data.
```

Because a figure can move, never write "the figure below". Give it a `\label` and refer to it by number with `\ref`. Inside the environment, `\centering` centers the content without the extra vertical space that a `center` environment would add.

In Eneles, press `Shift + Space`, open **Structure**, and choose **Figure** to insert this skeleton.

## Placement specifiers

The letters in the square brackets are permissions, not commands. They tell LaTeX where the figure is allowed to go.

| Specifier | Allows the figure |
| --- | --- |
| `h` | Here, where it appears in the source, if it fits. |
| `t` | At the top of a page. |
| `b` | At the bottom of a page. |
| `p` | On a separate page that holds only floats. |
| `!` | Relax the limits on how much of a page floats may fill. |

The order of the letters has no effect: `[htbp]` and `[pbth]` mean the same. LaTeX tries here first, then top, then bottom, then a float page. Without any brackets you get `[tbp]`.

Figures always appear in the order you wrote them. A figure that cannot be placed therefore holds back every later one, and they all pile up at the end of the chapter or document. That is the usual result of a strict `[h]` or an image that is too tall. Allow more positions with `[htbp]`, or flush the waiting figures onto their own pages with `\clearpage`.

To pin a figure exactly where you typed it, load the `float` package and use `[H]`. The figure then no longer floats, so a tall one can leave a large gap at the bottom of the previous page.

## Caption first, then label

`\label` records the last number that was assigned before it. Inside a figure that number comes from `\caption`, so the label must follow the caption. Put it earlier and it picks up the section number instead.

```latex-example
\setcounter{section}{3}
\section{Results}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \label{fig:early}
  \includegraphics[width=2.5cm]{example-image}
  \caption{The apparatus.}
  \label{fig:late}
\end{figure}

Early label: \ref{fig:early}. Late label: \ref{fig:late}.
```

Place the caption below the image for figures. That is the convention, and the standard classes space it for that position.

## Side-by-side figures

For parts of one figure, load `subcaption`. Each `subfigure` takes a width and gets its own letter and caption, and the outer `\caption` covers the whole. The `geometry` line only makes the page small enough to show here.

```latex-document
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage[paperwidth=12cm,paperheight=9cm,margin=8mm]{geometry}
\usepackage{graphicx}
\usepackage{subcaption}
\pagestyle{empty}
\begin{document}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{subfigure}{0.45\linewidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{example-image-a}
    \caption{Before}
    \label{fig:before}
  \end{subfigure}
  \hfill
  \begin{subfigure}{0.45\linewidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{example-image-b}
    \caption{After}
    \label{fig:after}
  \end{subfigure}
  \caption{The sample before and after polishing.}
  \label{fig:polish}
\end{figure}

Compare \ref{fig:before} with \ref{fig:after}.
\end{document}
```

For two independent figures that share a row, each with its own number, no package is needed. Put each image and caption in a `minipage`.

```latex-example
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{example-image-a}
    \caption{Front view.}
  \end{minipage}\hfill
  \begin{minipage}{0.47\linewidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{example-image-b}
    \caption{Side view.}
  \end{minipage}
\end{figure}
```

<Callout type="warning" title="No blank line between the two halves">
  A blank line between two `subfigure` or `minipage` blocks starts a new paragraph, so the second one drops below the first. Separate them with `\hfill` or nothing at all, and make sure the widths add up to less than `\linewidth`.
</Callout>

## List of figures

`\listoffigures` prints every caption with its number and page. A long caption makes a clumsy entry, so give a short version in square brackets for the list.

```latex-example
\listoffigures

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=2cm]{example-image}
  \caption[Sensor layout]{Layout of the twelve
    sensors on the north wall, seen from inside.}
\end{figure}
```

The list is built from the previous compile, so it needs two passes to be up to date. See [Contents, lists, and indexes](/learn/contents-lists-and-indexes).

## Common mistakes

- **`\label` before `\caption`, or outside the figure.** No error, but `\ref` prints the section number. Move the label after the caption.
- **Only `[h]`.** LaTeX reports ``LaTeX Warning: `h' float specifier changed to `ht'`` and may still push the figure pages later. Write `[htbp]`.
- **Many figures and little text.** Figures queue up until TeX stops with `! LaTeX Error: Too many unprocessed floats.` Add `\clearpage` where the queue should be emptied.
- **`\caption` outside a float.** `! LaTeX Error: \caption outside float.` A caption needs a `figure` or `table` around it.
