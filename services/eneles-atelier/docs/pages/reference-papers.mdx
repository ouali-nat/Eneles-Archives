# Reference papers

Attach papers from arXiv to a project and keep their LaTeX source beside your own work. You can read a paper's source, open the paper in the editor to compile it, and have the assistant use it as context.

<Callout type="note" title="Not the bibliography">
  A reference paper is a paper's source kept with your project. To add a paper to your `.bib` file and cite it, see [References and citations](/docs/references).
</Callout>

## Attach a paper

Open the **References** panel in the editor. If it is not on screen, choose **View**, then **Panels**, then **References**.

1. Type the paper's arXiv ID in the box at the top of the panel, for example `1706.03762`. An `arXiv:` prefix or a link to the paper's page on arxiv.org is accepted too.
2. Click the plus button (**Attach paper**), or press `Enter`.

The panel reports "Fetching from arXiv", then counts the files as it saves them. When it finishes, the paper is in the list with its files showing.

Attaching a paper needs edit access to the project. Two things can stop it:

- **The paper has several versions.** The panel says how many and asks you to name one. Add `v` and the version number to the ID, as in `1706.03762v7`, and attach again.
- **"That paper is already attached."** The project already has a paper with that ID.

## What is stored

Eneles downloads the paper's source from arXiv and stores all of it with your project, in the same way as an [arXiv import](/docs/importing#from-arxiv):

- Text files, such as `.tex`, `.bib`, and `.sty` files, are stored as text.
- Figures and other files are stored as they are. They count toward the [upload storage](/docs/projects-and-files#size-limits-and-upload-storage) of the person who attached the paper.
- The paper's main document is found automatically, saved as `main.tex`, and marked **main** in the list.

The same files are left out as in an import, such as a file larger than 10 MB. If any were, Eneles says "Attached, but" with the number of files left out, and gives the reason for the first.

The paper is kept apart from your own files. It is not in the **Files** panel, and it is not compiled with your project.

## Read a paper's source

Click a paper in the panel to show or hide its files. Click a text file to read it: the file opens in a dialog that you can scroll but not edit.

- **Copy source** copies the whole file.
- **Open paper in the editor** opens the paper in a new tab, as described below.

Figures and other files that are not text are grayed out in the list. They open with the paper in the editor.

## Open a paper in the editor

Hover over a paper and click the arrow button (**Open in the editor to compile**). The paper opens in a new tab as a project of its own, with the full editor, so you can compile it and see its PDF.

Next to the title, **Reference for** and the name of your project links back to the project the paper is attached to. The back arrow at the left end of the toolbar goes there too.

Access to the paper follows your project. Anyone who can open your project can open the paper, and anyone who can edit your project can edit the paper's files. A reference paper cannot have reference papers of its own.

## Remove a paper

Hover over a paper and click the bin button (**Remove reference**). Click **Remove** to confirm, or **Keep** to leave it.

Removing a paper deletes the stored copy and frees the storage its figures used. Changes made to the paper's files in the editor go with it. Deleting a project deletes its reference papers too.

## Limits and permissions

- A project can hold up to 30 reference papers.
- Attaching and removing papers needs edit access. Everyone who can open the project can see the papers, read their source, and open them in the editor.
- Reference papers do not appear on the [Projects](/projects) page.
- Reference papers are not part of [GitHub sync](/docs/github-sync). A push sends only your project's own files.
- Reference papers are not in the ZIP you get from **Download ZIP**.

## The assistant and reference papers

When you ask the [assistant](/docs/assistant) for something in a project with papers attached, it can read the text files of those papers. That makes requests such as "use the notation from the attached paper" possible.

The assistant only reads reference papers. It never proposes a change to one: every change it proposes is to your project's own files.

With many long papers attached, the assistant is given their text files up to a total of about two million characters, starting with the paper that was attached first.
